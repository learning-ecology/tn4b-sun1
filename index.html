📖 Grammar in Context (Page 63)

Read and listen to Nate and Maizy's conversation

💬 Advert Objects Conversation

Nate: OK, I think the first one shows those things on top of a cooker where you cook food.

Maizy: No, they can't be. Look at the colour. They must be buttons. It could be a gadget, but I don't know exactly what it is.

Nate: Hmm. The second one might be a piece of kitchen equipment, but I'm not sure.

Maizy: Mmm, it may be.

Notice the Modal Verbs:

🔴 can't → Maizy thinks cooker top is impossible.

🟡 could / might / may → Nate and Maizy think it is possible, but they are not 100% sure.

🟢 must → Maizy feels very certain they are buttons.

🤔 How Sure Are You?

Analyzing meaning and degrees of certainty

"It might be a piece of kitchen equipment."

Question: Is the speaker 100% sure?

❌

IMPOSSIBLE

You think it cannot be true.

can't
❓

POSSIBLE

You are not sure.

could / may / might
✅

VERY LIKELY

You are almost certain.

must

"It could / may / might be a mobile phone, but I'm not sure."

Very Likely / Almost Certain

💡 Tiếng Việt: Hẳn là / Chắc chắn là...

📐 Grammar Form

Modal verb + bare infinitive (without "to")

Subject + [ modal verb ] + infinitive (without "to")

Example: It + might + be a mobile.

❌ Common Mistakes

  • ❌ "It might to be a mobile."
    (Do NOT add 'to')
  • ❌ "It must is a ring."
    (Use bare verb 'be', not 'is/are')
  • ❌ "They can't are buttons."

✅ Correct Sentences

  • ✅ "It might be a mobile."
  • ✅ "It must be a ring."
  • ✅ "They can't be buttons."

📄 Listening Transcript

Teacher-controlled view of interview script

Interviewer: Today on Smart Shopping, we are talking to retail expert James Lewis. James, how do shopping centres make us spend more money?

James: Well, first, layout is key. Shopping centres are designed so you stay as long as possible. The escalators are placed far apart so you have to walk past many shop windows!

Interviewer: What about product placement on shelves?

James: Most expensive items are on shelves at eye level, where you see them first. Special offers are usually placed near the entrance on the right because people naturally look right!

📖 Reading: Why We Buy Unplanned Items

Short A2+ passage

Many shoppers enter a shopping centre planning to buy only one item, but leave with three! Why does this happen? Stores place expensive items on shelves at eye level where customers look first. Bright lights near the shop window and special offers at the front right also attract attention. When people feel relaxed, they buy more!
